Project · Podcast

#HerHousing

A podcast on today’s housing issues with a gendered focus — and a website of vetted housing resources for women.

#HerHousing brand illustration

Why this show

The starting point is Matthew Desmond’s Evicted: Poverty and Profit in an American City and the Eviction Lab that grew out of it — 83 million eviction records documenting how widespread housing insecurity is, in cities and small towns alike.

Inside that data is a pattern that rarely leads the coverage. Women are the group most severely affected: poor women of colour, survivors of domestic violence, and women with children all carry a higher risk of eviction. And Black women are evicted at higher rates than any other group.

The reasons compound. Lower wages from the pay gap. Living paycheck to paycheck. Primary responsibility for raising children. A rent burden that falls disproportionately on women. And the gender power dynamic between mostly male landlords and female tenants.

Education does not protect you

Why it matters to her

In 2015, working full time in advertising while doing her M.A. in Women’s History full time, she found a bedbug in her apartment. She reported it to her landlord. His response was that she needed to clean her apartment.

She fought him. Because she spoke up, she was retaliated against — and eventually evicted.

Pursuing the legal battle is what showed her the gap this show exists to fill: plenty of outlets and organizations cover housing, but very few cover it through a gendered, intersectional lens. The data that does exist concentrates on poor women, which leaves out the broader truth — that the disparity holds at every level of education and class.

The show

Format

Part scripted, with banter, recurring segments and interviews. She hosts. Largely self-contained, with studio sessions for interviews and live recording at events.

Scripted · Segments · Interviews · Live

Who speaks

Women who have been through the dark side of housing, telling their own stories — alongside policymakers, activists, artists, mental health advocates, lawyers, disability advocates, seniors and scholars.

First-person · Expert · Cross-sector

Audience

Women first, and with them policymakers, students, activists, housing stakeholders, and religious, educational, legal and housing institutions.

Advocacy · Policy · Academic

The resource list

A companion website listing housing resources for women — every one of them vetted. The show builds the network; the site makes it usable.

Vetted resources · Referral network
